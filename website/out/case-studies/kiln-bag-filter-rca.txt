Diagnostic found undersized ductwork causing inadequate draft and excessive inlet velocities.</p><p style="font-size: 14px; color: #5a6e58; line-height: 1.85; margin-bottom: 1.5rem;">---</p><h1 style="font-family: Georgia, serif; font-size: 2rem; color: #1a2e1a; font-weight: normal; margin-top: 2rem; margin-bottom: 1rem; line-height: 1.2;">10 TPH Kiln — Furnace Backfire RCA</h1><p style="font-size: 14px; color: #5a6e58; line-height: 1.85; margin-bottom: 1.5rem;">[Diagnostic Chart: Problem Timeline → Investigation → Root Cause → Solution → Results]</p><h2 style="font-family: Georgia, serif; font-size: 1.5rem; color: #1a2e1a; font-weight: normal; margin-top: 2rem; margin-bottom: 1rem; line-height: 1.2;">The Problem</h2><p style="font-size: 14px; color: #5a6e58; line-height: 1.85; margin-bottom: 1.5rem;"><strong>Symptoms</strong> — 2–3 furnace backfires per week, 30–60 min downtime each. Visible stack emissions. Burner replaced twice without solving issue. Operating at 60% capacity due to unreliability.</p><p style="font-size: 14px; color: #5a6e58; line-height: 1.85; margin-bottom: 1.5rem;"><strong>Root Cause</strong> — Ductwork severely undersized. Two 600mm ducts consuming 85% of induced-draft fan capacity, causing system imbalance and poor combustion.</p><h2 style="font-family: Georgia, serif; font-size: 1.5rem; color: #1a2e1a; font-weight: normal; margin-top: 2rem; margin-bottom: 1rem; line-height: 1.2;">Technical Diagnosis</h2><p style="font-size: 14px; color: #5a6e58; line-height: 1.85; margin-bottom: 1.5rem;"><strong>Gas Volume Calculation:</strong></p><ul style="margin-bottom: 1.5rem; margin-left: 1.5rem; color: #5a6e58;"><li style="margin-bottom: 0.5rem; line-height: 1.7;">Feedstock: 2.5 m³/min</li><li style="margin-bottom: 0.5rem; line-height: 1.7;">Combustion products: 3.5 m³/min  </li><li style="margin-bottom: 0.5rem; line-height: 1.7;">Moisture evaporation: 1.2 m³/min</li><li style="margin-bottom: 0.5rem; line-height: 1.7;"><strong>Total: 432 m³/hr needed</strong></li></ul><p style="font-size: 14px; color: #5a6e58; line-height: 1.85; margin-bottom: 1.5rem;"><strong>Ductwork Problem:</strong></p><ul style="margin-bottom: 1.5rem; margin-left: 1.5rem; color: #5a6e58;"><li style="margin-bottom: 0.5rem; line-height: 1.7;">Main duct: 400mm diameter (0.126 m² area)</li><li style="margin-bottom: 0.5rem; line-height: 1.7;">Face velocity: 57 m/s (should be 15–20 m/s)</li><li style="margin-bottom: 0.5rem; line-height: 1.7;">System pressure drop: 790 mm WC (fan rated for only 400 mm WC)</li><li style="margin-bottom: 0.5rem; line-height: 1.7;">Result: Induced draft pulling air through furnace at excessive speed → incomplete combustion → carbon buildup → backfire</li></ul><h2 style="font-family: Georgia, serif; font-size: 1.5rem; color: #1a2e1a; font-weight: normal; margin-top: 2rem; margin-bottom: 1rem; line-height: 1.2;">The Solution</h2><p style="font-size: 14px; color: #5a6e58; line-height: 1.85; margin-bottom: 1.5rem;">1. <strong>Enlarge ducts</strong> from 400mm to 1000–1200mm (reduces velocity to 12 m/s) 2. <strong>Install cyclone</strong> before baghouse for coarse dust separation 3. <strong>Upgrade fan</strong> to rated capacity with soft-start 4. <strong>Optimize pulse cleaning</strong> — trigger at 120–130 mm WC differential pressure 5. <strong>Reduce ID fan speed</strong> post-fix: 47 Hz → 25–32 Hz = 74% energy savings</p><h2 style="font-family: Georgia, serif; font-size: 1.5rem; color: #1a2e1a; font-weight: normal; margin-top: 2rem; margin-bottom: 1rem; line-height: 1.2;">Results</h2><p style="font-size: 14px; color: #5a6e58; line-height: 1.85; margin-bottom: 1.5rem;">✓ <strong>Zero backfires</strong> in first 100 operating hours   ✓ <strong>Stack emissions eliminated</strong> — 80% reduction visible   ✓ <strong>Throughput recovered</strong> — 6 TPH → 9.8 TPH consistent   ✓ <strong>Energy savings</strong> — 74% fan power reduction = ₹1,80,000/yr   ✓ <strong>ROI</strong> — 8-month payback on ₹4,50,000 investment</p><p style="font-size: 14px; color: #5a6e58; line-height: 1.85; margin-bottom: 1.5rem;">---</p><p style="font-size: 14px; color: #5a6e58; line-height: 1.85; margin-bottom: 1.5rem;"><strong>Key Lesson</strong>: Equipment failures are often system design problems, not component failures.
