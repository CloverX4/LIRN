3:T3171,<h1 style="font-family: Georgia, serif; font-size: 2rem; color: #1a2e1a; font-weight: normal; margin-top: 2rem; margin-bottom: 1rem; line-height: 1.2;">Pressure Loss Budget & Calculations</h1><h2 style="font-family: Georgia, serif; font-size: 1.5rem; color: #1a2e1a; font-weight: normal; margin-top: 2rem; margin-bottom: 1rem; line-height: 1.2;">System design from first principles</h2><p style="font-size: 14px; color: #5a6e58; line-height: 1.85; margin-bottom: 1.5rem;">Complete pressure loss budgeting for dust collection systems. Calculate stack gas volumes, duct velocities, and component resistances with accuracy. Prevents oversized fans and system inefficiencies.</p><p style="font-size: 14px; color: #5a6e58; line-height: 1.85; margin-bottom: 1.5rem;">[Diagram: PLB Flow — Kiln Outlet → Duct Friction → Cyclone → Baghouse → Stack Exit]</p><h2 style="font-family: Georgia, serif; font-size: 1.5rem; color: #1a2e1a; font-weight: normal; margin-top: 2rem; margin-bottom: 1rem; line-height: 1.2;">What We Calculate</h2><p style="font-size: 14px; color: #5a6e58; line-height: 1.85; margin-bottom: 1.5rem;"><strong>Gas Volume</strong> — Feedstock intake + combustion products + evaporated moisture <strong>Duct Losses</strong> — Friction + velocity head in ductwork <strong>Component Drops</strong> — Cyclone, baghouse, diplegs, transitions <strong>System Total</strong> — Sum to specify correct fan capacity <strong>Safety Margin</strong> — Typically +10% for fouling, aging</p><p style="font-size: 14px; color: #5a6e58; line-height: 1.85; margin-bottom: 1.5rem;">Total system pressure loss must equal the static pressure your fan can produce. If you underestimate pressure loss, you specify an undersized fan and airflow falls short. If you overestimate, you waste energy and money.</p><p style="font-size: 14px; color: #5a6e58; line-height: 1.85; margin-bottom: 1.5rem;">Pressure loss budget is the engineering foundation for fan selection, energy consumption estimation, and troubleshooting inadequate airflow.</p><h2 style="font-family: Georgia, serif; font-size: 1.5rem; color: #1a2e1a; font-weight: normal; margin-top: 2rem; margin-bottom: 1rem; line-height: 1.2;">Key Parameters</h2><p style="font-size: 14px; color: #5a6e58; line-height: 1.85; margin-bottom: 1.5rem;"><strong>Stack Gas Volume (m³/hr at operating temperature)</strong></p><p style="font-size: 14px; color: #5a6e58; line-height: 1.85; margin-bottom: 1.5rem;">This is the actual volume of gas generated by your process at operating temperature, not at standard conditions. For a 1000°C kiln exhaust, the volume is much larger at the kiln exit than at ambient temperature.</p><p style="font-size: 14px; color: #5a6e58; line-height: 1.85; margin-bottom: 1.5rem;">Calculate using: $$V_{actual} = \frac{V_{mass} \times T_{actual}}{T_{ref}}$$</p><p style="font-size: 14px; color: #5a6e58; line-height: 1.85; margin-bottom: 1.5rem;">Where V_mass is the mass flow rate, T_actual is operating temperature in Kelvin, T_ref is reference temperature (usually 273 K for 0°C).</p><p style="font-size: 14px; color: #5a6e58; line-height: 1.85; margin-bottom: 1.5rem;"><strong>Face Velocity & Duct Diameter</strong></p><p style="font-size: 14px; color: #5a6e58; line-height: 1.85; margin-bottom: 1.5rem;">Face velocity is air speed in a duct (m/s). Diameter is chosen to maintain face velocity in optimal range:</p><ul style="margin-bottom: 1.5rem; margin-left: 1.5rem; color: #5a6e58;"><li style="margin-bottom: 0.5rem; line-height: 1.7;">Hard, non-abrasive particles: 15-20 m/s</li><li style="margin-bottom: 0.5rem; line-height: 1.7;">Moderately abrasive minerals: 12-18 m/s</li><li style="margin-bottom: 0.5rem; line-height: 1.7;">Highly abrasive dusts: 10-15 m/s</li></ul><p style="font-size: 14px; color: #5a6e58; line-height: 1.85; margin-bottom: 1.5rem;">Calculate duct diameter: $$D = \sqrt{\frac{4V_{volume}}{π \times V_{face} \times 3600}}$$</p><h2 style="font-family: Georgia, serif; font-size: 1.5rem; color: #1a2e1a; font-weight: normal; margin-top: 2rem; margin-bottom: 1rem; line-height: 1.2;">Component Pressure Losses</h2><p style="font-size: 14px; color: #5a6e58; line-height: 1.85; margin-bottom: 1.5rem;"><strong>Inlet Entry Losses</strong> Air entering a duct from a large chamber experiences entry loss. A well-designed inlet (gradual bell shape) has entry coefficient 0.04-0.10. A sharp-edged inlet has coefficient 0.5 or higher.</p><p style="font-size: 14px; color: #5a6e58; line-height: 1.85; margin-bottom: 1.5rem;">Pressure loss: $$ΔP = K \times \frac{ρ \times V^2}{2}$$</p><p style="font-size: 14px; color: #5a6e58; line-height: 1.85; margin-bottom: 1.5rem;"><strong>Ductwork Friction</strong> Friction loss depends on duct length, diameter, surface roughness, and flow rate.</p><p style="font-size: 14px; color: #5a6e58; line-height: 1.85; margin-bottom: 1.5rem;">Using Darcy equation: $$ΔP = f \times \frac{L}{D} \times \frac{ρ \times V^2}{2}$$</p><p style="font-size: 14px; color: #5a6e58; line-height: 1.85; margin-bottom: 1.5rem;">Where f is Darcy friction factor. For commercial steel ducts with standard roughness, f ≈ 0.02-0.03.</p><p style="font-size: 14px; color: #5a6e58; line-height: 1.85; margin-bottom: 1.5rem;"><strong>Bends & Elbows</strong> Each 90° elbow adds pressure loss equivalent to 0.3-0.6 duct diameters of straight duct (loss coefficient 0.3-0.6). 45° bends have lower loss (0.15-0.30). Long-radius elbows have lower loss than short-radius.</p><p style="font-size: 14px; color: #5a6e58; line-height: 1.85; margin-bottom: 1.5rem;"><strong>Transitions & Expansions</strong> Expanding ductwork (increasing diameter) converts dynamic pressure to static pressure but causes separation and turbulence. Gradual transitions (7-10° included angle) recover 60-80% of dynamic pressure. Abrupt expansions recover only 20-30%.</p><p style="font-size: 14px; color: #5a6e58; line-height: 1.85; margin-bottom: 1.5rem;">Contracting sections (decreasing diameter) cause additional loss at the vena contracta.</p><p style="font-size: 14px; color: #5a6e58; line-height: 1.85; margin-bottom: 1.5rem;"><strong>Filter/Collector Resistance</strong> Baghouse pressure drop depends on filter area and face velocity. New, clean bags have DP = 75-100 mm WC at rated velocity (0.75-1.0 m/min). Dust buildup increases DP progressively until cleaning is triggered at 150-200 mm WC.</p><p style="font-size: 14px; color: #5a6e58; line-height: 1.85; margin-bottom: 1.5rem;">For an 8 m² baghouse collection area with 2000 m³/hr flow:</p><ul style="margin-bottom: 1.5rem; margin-left: 1.5rem; color: #5a6e58;"><li style="margin-bottom: 0.5rem; line-height: 1.7;">Face velocity = 2000 ÷ (8 × 60) = 4.17 m/min = 0.07 m/s</li><li style="margin-bottom: 0.5rem; line-height: 1.7;">Clean DP ≈ 100 mm WC (typical for this design)</li></ul><p style="font-size: 14px; color: #5a6e58; line-height: 1.85; margin-bottom: 1.5rem;">Cyclone pressure drop: DP = 1.5-4.0 × dynamic pressure = 1.5-4.0 × ρV²/2</p><p style="font-size: 14px; color: #5a6e58; line-height: 1.85; margin-bottom: 1.5rem;"><strong>Fan Discharge & Stack Losses</strong> Air exiting the fan experiences discharge loss (typically 0.5-1.0 of dynamic pressure). Stack outlet velocity should be 10-15 m/s for proper plume dispersion and to prevent downwash.</p><h2 style="font-family: Georgia, serif; font-size: 1.5rem; color: #1a2e1a; font-weight: normal; margin-top: 2rem; margin-bottom: 1rem; line-height: 1.2;">Pressure Loss Budget Example</h2><p style="font-size: 14px; color: #5a6e58; line-height: 1.85; margin-bottom: 1.5rem;">Consider a industrial kiln dust collection system:</p><pre style="background: #1a3325; color: #c8bfa8; padding: 1rem; border-radius: 4px; overflow: auto; font-size: 12px; font-family: 'JetBrains Mono', monospace; margin-bottom: 1.5rem; line-height: 1.5;"><code>PRESSURE LOSS SUMMARY

A kiln exhaust at 200°C has different pressure drop characteristics than the same flow at 50°C in a filter.</p><p style="font-size: 14px; color: #5a6e58; line-height: 1.85; margin-bottom: 1.5rem;">Pressure drop scales linearly with density: $$ΔP_{cold} = ΔP_{hot} \times \frac{T_{cold}}{T_{hot}}$$</p><p style="font-size: 14px; color: #5a6e58; line-height: 1.85; margin-bottom: 1.5rem;">If your baghouse operates with air cooled to 50°C, pressure drop through bags increases compared to if the air arrived at 150°C.</p><h2 style="font-family: Georgia, serif; font-size: 1.5rem; color: #1a2e1a; font-weight: normal; margin-top: 2rem; margin-bottom: 1rem; line-height: 1.2;">Design Iterations</h2><p style="font-size: 14px; color: #5a6e58; line-height: 1.85; margin-bottom: 1.5rem;">Optimal system design requires balancing: 1. <strong>Duct sizing</strong> — Larger diameter = lower friction loss = smaller fan, but more materials cost 2. <strong>Filter area</strong> — Larger collection area = lower face velocity = lower DP, but higher capital cost 3. <strong>Fan horsepower</strong> — Inverse of efficiency; larger systems need proper fan selection 4. <strong>Operating cost</strong> — Energy consumption over 5-10 years often exceeds equipment cost</p><p style="font-size: 14px; color: #5a6e58; line-height: 1.85; margin-bottom: 1.5rem;">We typically iterate designs to minimize total life-cycle cost.</p><h2 style="font-family: Georgia, serif; font-size: 1.5rem; color: #1a2e1a; font-weight: normal; margin-top: 2rem;
