Buckets mounted on belt or chain scoop material from the boot section and discharge at the head.</p><p style="font-size: 14px; color: #5a6e58; line-height: 1.85; margin-bottom: 1.5rem;"><strong>Centrifugal Discharge</strong> — High-speed operation. Buckets fling material outward at the head pulley using centrifugal force. Best for free-flowing materials like grain, sand, and dry minerals where gentle handling is not critical.</p><p style="font-size: 14px; color: #5a6e58; line-height: 1.85; margin-bottom: 1.5rem;"><strong>Continuous Discharge</strong> — Low-speed operation. Buckets invert and material slides out under gravity over a back-plate. Required for fragile materials, lumpy products, or applications where breakage must be minimized.</p><h2 style="font-family: Georgia, serif; font-size: 1.5rem; color: #1a2e1a; font-weight: normal; margin-top: 2rem; margin-bottom: 1rem; line-height: 1.2;">Belt vs Chain Configuration</h2><p style="font-size: 14px; color: #5a6e58; line-height: 1.85; margin-bottom: 1.5rem;"><strong>Belt-mounted</strong> buckets use rubber or PVC belts as the carrying medium. Lighter, quieter operation with lower cost — standard for moderate temperatures and non-abrasive materials.</p><p style="font-size: 14px; color: #5a6e58; line-height: 1.85; margin-bottom: 1.5rem;"><strong>Chain-mounted</strong> buckets use single or double-strand roller chains. Required for high-temperature service (above 150°C), heavy lump materials, and applications with abrasive dust that would degrade a belt.</p><h2 style="font-family: Georgia, serif; font-size: 1.5rem; color: #1a2e1a; font-weight: normal; margin-top: 2rem; margin-bottom: 1rem; line-height: 1.2;">Boot & Head Section Design</h2><p style="font-size: 14px; color: #5a6e58; line-height: 1.85; margin-bottom: 1.5rem;">The boot section is where material enters and buckets scoop.
