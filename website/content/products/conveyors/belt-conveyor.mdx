## Flat vs Troughed Belt

Flat belt conveyors handle regular-shaped products and small-to-medium lump sizes. Simple design, easy cleaning, and lower cost make them ideal for clean manufactured products.

Troughed belts (35°, 45°, or 50° troughs) increase capacity and allow steeper inclines by containing material within the trough edges. Troughed design is essential for inclined transport and when material has varied lump sizes.

We custom-design belt profile and angle based on your material angle of repose and lump size distribution.

## Drive Systems

Direct-mounted gearbox drives are standard for conveyors up to 200 TPH. For longer conveyors or higher capacities, multiple-drive systems distribute load along the belt length, reducing belt tension and extending service life.

Soft-start systems or VFD drives are recommended for:
- Steep incline applications (reduces startup shock)
- Variable product feed rates
- Integration with process control systems
- Energy efficiency in variable-load operations

## Idler Design & Maintenance

Idlers are critical for belt support and tracking. We use:
- Return-side idlers every 1.5-2.0m for support
- Carry-side idlers every 3-5m for crown idler systems
- Wing pulleys on troughed conveyors to maintain proper trough geometry
- Steel roller bearings with grease-life specifications of 5000+ hours

Bearing failure is the primary cause of conveyor downtime. We specify sealed, pre-greased idlers that require maintenance every 1-2 years depending on duty.

## Belt Tensioning & Alignment

Proper belt tension is critical—too tight causes bearing wear, too loose causes slippage and premature failure. Initial tension is set using torque-wrench procedure on takeup bolts.

Belt alignment is maintained through:
- Crowned carry-side idlers to self-center the belt
- Automatic tracking idlers on return side
- Quarterly manual alignment checks
- Replacement of worn wings when belt wander exceeds ±10mm

Misaligned belts not only wear quickly but can fold and tear at the edge.

## Loading & Transfer Points

Chute design at loading points is critical to avoid:
- Material spillage at belt edges
- Excessive belt wear from impact
- Dust generation and fugitive emissions
- Belt mistracking from lopsided loading

We design custom loading chutes with:
- Velocity-matched inlet to prevent impact
- Longitudinal baffles to center material
- Sealed construction where required
- Easily removable wear plates

## Inclined Conveyor Considerations

Inclines above 10° require cleats (transverse ribs) to prevent material rollback and improve carrying capacity. Cleat height, spacing, and number are designed for your specific material friction coefficient.

Inclines above 20° require:
- Higher belt tension and stronger drive motors
- Cleated belt design
- Slope-load analysis for bearing and frame stress
- Redundant safety stops to prevent rollback if drive fails

We calculate conveyor horsepower using ISO 5048 equations accounting for material weight, friction, incline angle, and belt speed.

## Discharge & Cleanup

Belt scrapers remove residual material at the discharge end. In dusty applications, we install:
- Primary scraper at discharge point
- Secondary backup scraper to clear carry-side belt
- Belt washing station for hygiene-critical applications

All scrapers must be replaceable without crane work.

## Installation & Commissioning

Full installation service includes:
- Foundation cement pour verification with level/plumb checks
- Motor and gearbox alignment to ISO 23309 standards
- Belt splicing using vulcanized or metal splice methods
- Tension setting and tracking adjustment
- 40-hour run-in with no-load operation
- Graduated loading test to rated capacity
- Bearing temperature monitoring over first week

## Expected Service Life

With proper maintenance, belt conveyors deliver 5-10 years service:
- Steel structure: 15+ years
- Roller bearings: 5-7 years (idlers), 8-10 years (drive end)
- Rubber belt: 3-5 years depending on abrasiveness of material
- Cleats/scrapers: 1-3 years depending on wear material
